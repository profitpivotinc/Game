# 00 — THE FRAME
### The Skald in the Hall That Is Not What It Seems

*Canon anchors: C-FRAME-01 (saga recited ~Y+300), C-FRAME-02 (recitation and play diverge), C-FRAME-03 (each ending recontextualizes the frame), C-FRAME-04 (the reciter is never named), C-THEME-01 (word-fame), C-TONE-01/02/03 (verse form, epigraph, present-tense skald voice).*

This file is the outermost layer of VARGDROPI: the voice that opens the game and the four voices that close it. Everything the player plays is, in fiction, being sung tonight by a skald three hundred winters after the deeds — in a hall whose wrongness the player is meant to half-notice and never quite name until an ending names it for them. The hall's true nature (C-FRAME-03/04) is **seeded but never confirmed** here until the closings; the opening only lets the seams show.

Frame voice is **present tense, second person, addressed to the hall** (C-TONE-03). Played scenes elsewhere use standard script format. Stage directions and asides are bracketed and marked so the writer can tell the sung line from the staging.

---

## 0. Epigraph

*Composed in fornyrðislag flavor per C-TONE-01/02 — two stressed, alliterating syllables per half-line, no rhyme. Not a translation of any real verse. Spoken by the skald before the first strike of the hall-post; it states the theme (C-THEME-01) as a law, not a lament.*

> Fire will fail    and the fell go cold,
> the hall come down    to hearth-cold ash;
> but the word once carved    wakes the carver's ghost —
> a name outlasts    the naming mouth.
>
> Ravens count    the reddened dead,
> the wound-sea    washes the slain;
> yet who is sung    and who is silenced
> one hand holds:    the hand at the haft.

*[ASIDE — design: the last half-line ("the hand at the haft") is the whole game in four words. The haft is the carving-knife's, not the axe's. Whoever holds the knife that cuts the runes decides which dead are remembered. Callback target for all four closings.]*

## 1. The Opening Recitation

*This is the first thing the player hears. It runs under and around the prologue. The four beats below map to the four opening beats in the Phase-1 packet; deliver them in order, letting play interrupt the song rather than the song pausing for play.*

---

### Beat 1 — The Theme, Struck on the Post

**[STAGE: darkness; a single hearth. The SKALD strikes the hall-post once. He does not give his own name, now or ever — C-FRAME-04. He addresses the benches directly, in present tense.]**

> **SKALD:** Quiet, then. Quiet in the hall.
>
> You know the old truth, and I will not soften it: the herd goes down, the hearth-kin go down, and a man goes down after his own — and the one thing that does not die is the doom said over a dead man's deeds. The word outlives the wound. Remember that before I begin, for tonight the word is yours to hold. I sing you a man with four deaths in him and only one body, and before the fire burns low **you** will judge which of the four is the one worth carving.
>
> His name was Grím. Men called him the Masked.

*[ASIDE: establishes word-fame as the frame's opening move (C-THEME-01) and hands the audience — the player — the carver's authority. "Four deaths, one body" seeds the four endings without naming them. The Hávamál cadence is composed, never quoted (C-TONE-02).]*

---

### Beat 2 — The First Hedge, and the First Lie Sung as Truth

> **SKALD:** I sing it as it is carved. I sing it true.
>
> *(a beat; drier)* — though the carving and the cutting are not always the same knife.
>
> Hear how it is carved, then. Grím wore the half-mask for the fire that ate his face when he was a boy — a longhouse burning, a bairn pulled from under a falling beam, and the flames' thanks written on him forever. So the saga says. So I say, for the saga taught me.

**[STAGE: the played prologue is built to contradict this. In a Chapter 1 beat the mask comes off, and there are no burns — or there is something the scene will not let the narrator name (C-GRIM-02). The skald above states the burn-story as settled fact; the player will shortly see it is not.]**

*[ASIDE: this is the "saga remembers it differently" device (C-FRAME-02) planted in the first minutes, and its first entry in the Discrepancies table below. The skald is not lying by his own lights — he is reciting Grím's own cover story, which no witness ever contradicted, so it hardened into record. Word-fame remembers what was witnessed, not what was so.]*

---

### Beat 3 — The Face He Will Not Name, and the Hall's Quiet Wrongness

> **SKALD:** And what is under the mask?
>
> *(leans in, as if to tell)* — I will not tell you. Not because I keep a secret. Because the saga does not **know**, and any skald who tells you he knows is selling you something. Mark that, if you mark nothing else tonight: when a man is certain about a face he never saw, ask who paid him to be certain.

**[STAGE — the hall's deniable wrongness, seeded low so the player half-notices and cannot prove (C-WORLD-02): the mead in the horns never quite warms. The hearth-fire throws light but no smoke. The faces on the far benches do not age between verses, and no one rises to leave — none of them can quite find the door. And once, only once, the skald lets it slip:]**

> **SKALD:** I have sung this before. *(smaller)* I will sing it again.

*[ASIDE: NONE of the wrongness is explained here. It reads, at worst, as the eeriness of a very old hall and a tired singer. The mead, the smokeless fire, the ageless benches, the unfindable door, the "again" — each is a thread an ending will pull (C-FRAME-03). Do not let the skald react to any of it; he is inside it and does not know.]*

---

### Beat 4 — The Name the Law Erased

> **SKALD:** Now. His mother.
>
> His mother was a queen, and her name was —
>
> *(the skald stops. His mouth works. Nothing comes.)*
>
> — her whom the law unnamed. I cannot give you what the Thing struck out. Three hundred winters gone to ash, the king dead, the court dead, the very kingdom a word in an old man's mouth — and still the ruling holds my tongue. That is what a carved word can do. It can outlive everyone it wronged and go on wronging them in the dark, in a hall where no one left alive was ever her enemy.
>
> They called what was done to her a *níð*. A poem. Her house was not burned down. It was **spoken** down. Keep that, too: in this saga the deadliest blade is a verse, and the man who carries it never washes blood from his hands, because there never is any.

*[ASIDE: erasure outliving death, demonstrated in the frame itself (C-THEME-01). The blank where "Aldís" should be is load-bearing — the player will later hear the name spoken by the three the law's redaction cannot silence: an outlaw (Gunnhild), the níð's own heir who keeps the naming stave (Rúnolfr), and the dead (Salgerð) — see Discrepancy 3. Do not let the skald ever say "Aldís." The tongue-lock is canon (C-GRIM-03, and the frame-level demonstration of the níð device).]*

---

**[STAGE: the hall-post is struck a second time. The prologue proper begins. The song recedes under the played scene, to return only at the chapter breaks and at the end the player earns.]**

## 2. The Four Closings

*Exactly one of these is reached, chosen by the ledgers and gates in `07-endings.md` / `08-branch-map.md` (C-END-01). Each takes the SAME hall from the opening and turns the lamp differently on it. The wrongness seeded in Beat 3 is the same wrongness in all four — only the amount of light changes. Deliver each in the same present-tense voice, addressed to the hall.*

*Design law for the set (C-FRAME-03): **the four endings differ only in how far the telling lets you SEE what the hall is.** King of Men lets you see least (it looks like an honest old mead-hall). The Successor lets you see everything (it is the maw). Benevolent God and Merciless Ruler sit between — one warm, one gilded, both untrue in different directions.*

---

### 2A. Closing — KING OF MEN
*Gate (C-END-01): Sung mercy ≥ +40 AND man_blood ≥ +30 AND Hjortdal trust ≥ 60 AND trial arc complete AND final choice "sever the blood." Grím gives up all Wyrd for the last act — he chose to be only a man, and to be judged by law under Skapti [P] rather than by power.*

**[STAGE: the SKALD sets down the harp. For the length of this closing — and only this closing — recitation and play agree. He does not perform. He reports.]**

> **SKALD:** So he cut it out of himself. The thread the Norns could not fix, he severed with his own hand, and stood in the law-circle a man like any of you — no fate-craft left in him, nothing to bend, only a name and the nerve to answer for it. And the Lawspeaker, who once spoke a queen out of the world, spoke her son back into it. Law used once as a murder-knife, made once to mend. It does not undo the murder. It is only the first time the knife cut the other way.
>
> *(and here the hall's strangeness thins — for one breath the mead in the horns almost warms, and a man on the far bench half-rises and finds, for the first time, where the door is; it is only the honest strangeness of a very old room, and the folk on the benches are freed folk, thralls' grandchildren, keeping a true-ish memory as well as memory keeps.)*
>
> Dag was crowned regardless. The sun rose on its rails, as it always does, in every telling of this I have ever sung. But under that fixed sun a man chose to be remembered **smaller**, and truer, and that is rarer than a crown.
>
> *(a pause; then, plainly, not as flourish)* — and there were deeds that night no witness lived to carry to me. I will name them anyway, though I sing them thin, from the little that came down. For a record with no gaps is a record that killed its witnesses, and I would rather hand you a true thing with holes in it than a whole thing that is a lie.

*[ASIDE: even the best ending confesses the record is partial — True > Sung (C-ALIGN-02). The skald voluntarily names the unwitnessed deeds, the opposite of the Merciless Ruler's carver, who is paid to omit them. The hall's wrongness reads as mere age here; it is NOT resolved or explained, only quieted. This is the ending that lets you see least — which is the point of C-FRAME-03.]*

---

### 2B. Closing — BENEVOLENT GOD
*Gate (C-END-01, per OQ-3): True mercy ≥ +40 AND man_blood ≤ −30 AND Hel arc complete. Grím takes the empty seat as god of the straw-dead — everyone Valhalla would not have.*

**[STAGE: the SKALD rises and turns UP the lamps. Light finds the far benches for the first time — and they are FULL. They were always full. The player simply could not see who sat there.]**

> **SKALD:** Look now. Look who has been listening.
>
> Every bench. The drowned and the fevered, the thralls worked into the ground, the stillborn and the forsworn, the ones no valkyrie stooped for because there was no glory on them — the straw-dead, all of them, the harvest that Valhalla turned away and the roots tried to swallow. And at the head of the hall, in the seat that stood empty three hundred years, a masked man who would not let them be eaten.
>
> This song was never a song. It is their evensong, and he is the one they sing it to. Hel's hall stood starving while two churches stole her dead; now it is fed, and full, and warm — the warmth of the grave made kind, which is a real warmth, and do not let anyone tell you it is not.

**[STAGE: at the door — which can now be seen — SALGERÐ "the Grey-Guest" [P] stands, and for the first time since the corpse-roads she is not walking. She sits. She rests.]**

> **SKALD:** The grey guest at the door has walked a long way to hear this. She does not walk tonight. Her petition is answered; her order is mended; the dead go where the dead are owed. Dag's crown is a footnote here, sung soft to the comforted dead. A people remembering the god who claimed them — the god who came down to the roots and, instead of feeding, **fed them**.

*[ASIDE: this hall is one of Hel's now-fed halls (C-LIE-06, C-EMISSARY-01). Salgerð finally resting closes her arc (C-ALIGN-02). It is genuinely tender AND genuinely a hall of the dead — Grím is a kind god of a grave, not a savior of the living world. Warm, and true, and still an ending under the roots. Lets you see MORE than King of Men: you see the benches are the dead. You do NOT yet see the maw.]*

---

### 2C. Closing — MERCILESS RULER
*Gate (C-END-01): True mercy_terror ≤ −40 AND man_blood ≥ +30 AND Sung mercy_terror ≥ 0 — the golden-age lie requires a clean PUBLIC ledger, which means the witnesses had to die.*

**[STAGE: the SKALD sings this one beautifully — the best fornyrðislag of the night, gold in every line. He performs harder than he has all evening. He is very good, and he is lying, and one small thing will give him away.]**

> **SKALD:** And so began the golden reign, and the land lay quiet under his hand as a field lies quiet under snow — and none who deserved mercy was ever denied it, and the harvests were fat, and the halls were loud, and the name of Grím the Masked was carved into every lintel from the fjord-mouth to the fells, and the folk blessed the mask that hid the face that kept them safe — and if the faces on the far benches never aged from verse to verse, why, the song had a gilding for that too: it painted them young, every one, and called their unchanging the peace's own blessing —

**[STAGE: beneath the sung line, the played record and the True Ledger show what the gold is painted over — the emptied holds, the yielding cut down where no one would carry the tale, the grið cried and refused in rooms with no survivors (C-ALIGN-03/04). The song and the record do not match. The song does not care.]**

> **SKALD:** — and blessed be the reign, and blessed the —
>
> *(the skald falters on a name. A witness he is naming cannot exist; that hold had no survivors, and he knows the tally, and for half a breath he hears himself. He files the name off mid-line and sings on, smoother now, having decided not to notice.)*
>
> — blessed the peace he made, world without wound, as it is carved, as I am paid to carve it.

*[ASIDE: the Sung ledger triumphant precisely because the witnesses were killed (C-ALIGN-03). The single impossible witness is the tell — a name that could not have lived to be quoted. Word-fame as propaganda: the hall applauds a slaughter it has been taught to call a reign. The skald is bought or compelled; the "as I am paid to carve it" is the closest he comes to confession. The hall's wrongness is gilded over here, not lifted — you see LESS than Benevolent God, because gold hides more than shadow.]*

---

### 2D. Closing — THE SUCCESSOR *(hidden)*
*Gate (C-END-01): True mercy_terror ≤ −40 AND man_blood ≤ −40 AND ≥5 einherjar CONSUMED rather than unhooked AND the concealed Ch3 corpse-road scene found. Grím does not destroy the Corpse-Eater — he replaces it. This is the only closing that tears the frame fully open.*

**[STAGE: no lamp is turned up. No harp is set down. Instead the seams the player half-saw all night — the mead that never warmed, the smokeless fire, the ageless faces, the door no one could find — tear. All of them. At once. And the SKALD's voice changes, because it was never only the skald's.]**

> **SKALD / THE VOICE:** You could not find the door because there is no door. You are not in a hall.
>
> You are in the mouth.
>
> These roots around you are the roots of the tree, and this hall is the thing beneath them, and it has been chewing since before your grandfather's grandfather was a story. The benches are full, as they are always full — but they are not an audience. They are the einherjar he fed here, the harvested, sat down to listen to the sound of their own eating. And the skald — the mouth that has sung to you all night, so warm, so trustworthy — the skald is the harvest **given a voice**, because a thing that eats the dead learns, in time, to sing them a reason.

**[STAGE: the truth of C-FRAME-04 lands — the reciter was never named because the reciter is not a person. And the truth of the whole game lands with it: "Valhalla" was the sales pitch; this hall is the slaughter-floor; every recitation was bait.]**

> **VOICE:** He did not kill the hunger. No one kills the hunger. He climbed into it and wore it, the way you wear a mask over a face you will not name — and now he is the thing at the roots, and I am his mouth, and you have been his meal all along, listening so politely to the tale of how you came to be eaten.
>
> And this is the last one. This is the last night. There is no field left to harvest, no living hall to sing in, no morning on any rail — I have told this before, and I will not tell it again, because when I carve the last line the roots part and there is nothing after. So lean close. It is almost —

**[STAGE: the last line is carved. The roots part. It ends. No next telling.]**

*[ASIDE: the frame's own promise from Beat 3 — "I will sing it again" — is revealed as the thing that finally cannot happen (C-FRAME-03/04). This is the deepest cut of C-THEME-01: whoever controls the carving controls which hall you believe you sat in, and here the carver was the hunger, so the whole saga was digestion wearing the shape of memory. The Successor consumes Salgerð's petition too — even Hel goes hungry (see `07-endings.md`). This closing lets you see EVERYTHING; it is the terminus of the see-more axis that orders all four.]*

## 4. Frame Rules of Thumb (for every downstream file)

1. **The skald is never named** (C-FRAME-04). Not in this file, not in any chapter break, not in stage directions. If a writer needs to refer to him, he is "the SKALD."
2. **The skald recites in present tense, to the hall** (C-TONE-03). Played scenes never adopt his voice; he never adopts theirs.
3. **The skald can be wrong, and does not know it** — except in the Merciless Ruler closing, where he knows and sings on anyway, and in the Successor closing, where "he" was never the one singing.
4. **Aldís's name is never in the skald's mouth.** It reaches the player only through Gunnhild, Rúnolfr, or Salgerð (Discrepancy 3).
5. **The hall's wrongness is shown, never explained, until a closing** (C-WORLD-02, C-FRAME-03). Seed the mead, the smokeless fire, the ageless benches, the missing door, the "again" — and let them lie there.
6. **Every new recited-vs-played gap gets a Discrepancies row in the same commit** (C-FRAME-02), citing the scene id.
7. **All names remain placeholders** (CANON §2); coined names keep **[P]** and blank final-name columns.
