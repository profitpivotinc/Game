# 02 — Character Dossiers

> This file is the game's cast reference: the *who* behind the world (`01-story-bible.md`) and the frame (`00-frame.md`). Numbers live in the `spec-*` sheets; scene scripts live in `03`–`07`. A dossier tells you who a person is, what they want, what they hide, and **when the player is allowed to learn it** — because in a game about word-fame, timing *is* character.

## How to read a dossier

Each entry carries: **Name** (placeholder) + Rename-Table label · **Role** · **Age** (at Y0) · **Faction / Allegiance** · **Appearance** · **Personality & Voice** · **Backstory** · **Arc / narrative function** · **Key relationships** · **Secrets & reveals** (what the player learns, and when).

**A guardrail that binds every dossier (KICKOFF §2, hard rules).** No one here is cartoon-evil and no one is noble-savage'd. The old faith hanged Yrsa; the new faith is right about the rot and wrong about the cure; the crown's erasures bought a real peace at a monstrous price. Villainy in VARGDROPI is *structural* — the hunger under both altars — and the people are only ever right, wrong, used, or grieving. **Only people earn loyalty** (C-LIE-04, C-LIE-05).

Roster below, in six groups: **The Two Threads** (Grím, Dag) · **The Erased Women** (Aldís, Yrsa, Ingunn) · **The Five Clan Leaders** · **The Crown & the Two Altars** (Ragnvald, Þorgeir, Bótólfr) · **The Dead's Petition** (Salgerð) · **The Landless** (Brandr).

# I. The Two Threads

## Grím "the Masked" [P]
*Rename-Table label: Grím ("the Masked") — protagonist.*

- **Role:** Protagonist — the Wyrdless (C-GRIM-01, C-GRIM-05).
- **Age:** ~19 at Y0.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** None. *Vargdropi* — legally kinless, barred by law from his own blood (C-GRIM-03). The whole game is the question of what allegiance he *chooses*.

**Appearance.** Lean, rope-muscled from the forge, a smith's burn-flecked forearms — the one part of the burn-story that is plainly true. He wears a **half-mask** of boiled leather over hardwood, covering brow to cheekbone on one side, that he says hides fire-scars (C-GRIM-02). Below it his mouth is unremarkable, quick to a crooked half-smile he uses like a shield. Dark hair, kept short and practical; grey eyes that hold too still. He moves with an economy that reads as trained long before anyone taught him — the uncanniness the saga later calls Wyrd. *The narrator never describes what is under the mask, because the narrator does not know* (C-GRIM-02, C-FRAME-02); the one Chapter 1 beat that lifts it refuses to resolve the image.

**Personality & Voice.** Terse, guarded, dry. He speaks less than everyone around him and deflects with a joke precisely when a truer man would open — the joke is a door he closes. He warms only in private, to few, and never for long. **He never gives the mask's reason the same way twice** (C-GRIM-02): a fire, a vow, a wager lost — the mask makes others fill his silences, and the saga fills them wrongly. His terseness is not stupidity; it is a boy who learned early that to be *named* is to be *unmade*, and who therefore refuses to hand the world enough of himself to carve.

**Backstory.** Born in the shadow of the Unmaking (Y−20…Y−19; Timeline §4): son of Queen **Aldís**, whose line was legally erased by commissioned *níð* alleging she lay with Loki (C-GRIM-03). The verse voided his paternity as a matter of law, which is *why it can never be confirmed* (C-GRIM-04) — the poem did not just slander a mother, it deleted a father. Fostered to a smith in a coastal hold, raised lowborn and safe precisely because he was nobody. Prodigious with every weapon he touches, uncannily so (C-GRIM-01) — and his Wyrd first manifested **involuntarily**, witnessed only as uncanny skill or impossible luck, **deniable in Chapter 1** (C-GRIM-05, C-WORLD-02); the saga names it *Wyrd* only in hindsight. Yet that witnessed uncanniness is what draws every faction's eye to him and reopens the question the crown paid to close.

**Arc / narrative function.** The one thread the Norns cannot fix (C-GRIM-05) — the in-fiction reason the game branches at all. His arc is two-beat: **discover what he is, then choose what it *means*** (C-GRIM-07) — by law (King of Men), by blood (Benevolent God), by terror (Merciless Ruler), or by hunger (The Successor). Mechanically his godlike skill is **meta, never stats** (C-GRIM-06, C-WYRD-01): Wyrd Points bend the Charge-Time system itself; he must never be an Orlandeau-style stat monster who trivializes the tactics. Every use of Wyrd in a *sung* battle pushes him toward Blood (C-WYRD-02) — his power is public, and skalds talk. He is the carver's blank stave: the player decides which of four deaths gets cut into him.

**Key relationships.**
- Son of **Aldís** — the absent center he is barred from claiming.
- Nephew of **Gunnhild** (Ulfheim), his maternal aunt — who wants his face on a banner, not her sister's son at her hearth.
- Tested by **Ragnvald** at ch1-08 (C-ROUTE-02): the legal father who disowned him orders him to burn his mother's kin — biological paternity unconfirmed (C-KING-02, C-GRIM-04).
- Foil and dark mirror to **Dag**: the free-born-nothing against the thrall-born-everything.
- Haunted in dreams by **Salgerð**, Hel's emissary (C-EMISSARY-01) — who may be a servant of his own kinswoman.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *Paternity (never resolved — C-GRIM-04).* Ragnvald or Loki. If the *níð* told true, **Hel is his kinswoman**, which is why her corpse-roads open to him and why two endings run through her (§ Hel, `01`). The player is *teased* both readings across Ch1–2 and is *never* handed an answer — the withholding is the point.
- *The mask.* What it hides is left open — burns, or something the story never verifies (C-GRIM-02). The Ch1 unmasking beat contradicts the sung burn-story (C-FRAME-02) without replacing it.
- *The Wyrdless nature.* Named and understood across Ch2–3; the einherjar returns (C-DEATH-02) are the proof made flesh — the harvest itself recognizes what he is.

## Dag [P]
*Rename-Table label: Dag — foil, thrall-born king-to-be.*

- **Role:** Foil — the fixed-fate king (Delita-analog) against whom every player-built ending is measured (C-DAG-01/02/03).
- **Age:** ~24 at Y0.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** New church (White God) → the King's service → the Crown. In every route his allegiance is *himself*, wearing whatever faith advances him.

**Appearance.** Tall, clean, deliberately unremarkable in the manner of a man who has learned that the thrall-born survive by being *legible* — no ornament that a free man could resent, no rag that marks the collar he was born in. He dresses a half-step below his station on purpose. A still, handsome, watchful face; a public smile he can hold for hours; eyes that are doing arithmetic while the mouth is warm. By Ch3–4 the White-God cross at his throat is plainer than any bishop's — a man who understood the optics of humility before he understood the theology.

**Personality & Voice.** **Warm in public, glacial in private.** He speaks in favors and ledgers, in the cadence of a man carving his own saga line by line and *aware he is being remembered*. He never raises his voice, because he never has to. Where Grím deflects to avoid being seen, Dag performs to control what is seen — the inverse mask, worn on the outside. His grief for Yrsa is genuine and is the *source* of his coldness, not its opposite: he loved someone the world found disposable, and drew the lesson that only power makes a person un-disposable.

**Backstory.** Thrall-born and brilliant. Converted after the old gods hanged his sister **Yrsa** in the last great blót (Y−6, C-DAG-01) — a thrall-born girl was an acceptable offering; a jarl's daughter was not (C-LIE-05). He entered the new faith through the door it holds open to exactly people like him — it *frees thralls* (C-LIE-04) — and rose through the church and Ragnvald's service on merit, patience, and a bottomless understanding of what people need to believe.

**Arc / narrative function.** **Crowned by endgame in every route** (C-DAG-02): his rise is authored, on rails, the fixed sun that rises no matter what the player does. He is the measuring stick. His only real question — *does he know what feeds under the church he built?* — resolves to **yes**: he learns it in Chapter 4 and **crowns himself anyway** (C-DAG-03), deciding a fed monster is a *governable* one and that a church which harvests cleanly is a better instrument than a grove that harvests savagely. He is grief matured into cold statecraft: the man fate *fixed*, standing beside the man fate *cannot* fix.

**Key relationships.**
- Brother of the hanged **Yrsa** — his engine of grief, and his private altar.
- Servant, then successor, of **Ragnvald** — he learns statecraft from the king's erasures and improves on them.
- Parallel and foil to **Grím** — fixed fate beside the Wyrdless; the two never stop being each other's answer.
- Wary patron, then user, of **Bótólfr** — the sincere cleric Dag deploys and, ultimately, discards.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *He stops believing by Ch3.* He no longer credits the martyr-story he preaches — he knows the relics feed something (C-LIE-03) — and perfects the church regardless, because it works. Revealed to the player across Ch3→4; confirmed in the Ch4 confrontation (`06`).
- *His coldness is love, inverted.* The Yrsa grief is real; the reveal that his warmth is manufactured and his grief is not recontextualizes every earlier scene.

# II. The Erased Women

> Three dossiers, **two women** — none of whom the player ever hears speak *living*. They survive only in others' carvings — the exact horror the game is about (C-THEME-01). Aldís is one erasure; **Yrsa and Ingunn are one woman erased twice** — hanged by the old faith, then sainted under a false name by the new (C-SAINT-01/02, **committed**). Ingunn keeps her own dossier because she is how the *cult* is presented and how the reveal is staged, but the body is Yrsa's. They make one argument: **word-fame overwrites the powerless, and both altars have blood** (C-LIE-05).

## Aldís [P]
*Rename-Table label: Aldís — Grím's mother, the unmade queen.*

- **Role:** The unmade queen — the crime the game is named for (C-GRIM-03, C-THEME-01).
- **Age:** Would be ~40s; **erased Y−20** (Timeline §4).
- **Faction / Allegiance:** Former royal house → the Ulfheim remnant is what her scattered kin became. In the present she has no allegiance because she has, in law, no existence.

**Appearance.** **Unfixable by design.** The player meets three incompatible portraits and no ground truth: the *níð*'s Aldís (a queen who lay with the Trickster — a slander carved to look like testimony); Ulfheim's Aldís (a wronged, radiant martyr-queen on a grief-banner); and Salgerð's Aldís (a figure glimpsed among the straw-dead, neither accusing nor absolving). Her "true" face is unrecoverable — that unrecoverability *is* the character (C-GRIM-04). Any file that renders her must render a *version*, flagged as someone's carving, never the woman.

**Personality & Voice.** **Never heard living.** She survives only in others' memory: in the poem that killed her, in Ulfheim's banner-verse, in a dead völva's dreams. A woman *spoken about*, never speaking — the purest expression of the theme. When "her voice" appears in any scene it must be quoted, framed, or dreamed, and marked as such.

**Backstory.** Wife and queen of King **Ragnvald**; sister of **Gunnhild**; mother of **Grím**. ~Twenty years before game start she was legally unmade by a commissioned *níð* alleging an affair with Loki (C-GRIM-03), carved by the Hrafnmark rune-lawyers (Rúnolfr's forebears) on the throne's coin and ratified at the Thing by lawspeaker **Skapti** (Timeline §4, C-THING-01). Her kin were outlawed and scattered into the Ulfheim remnant.

**Arc / narrative function.** **The absent center.** She does not have an arc; she is the hole every arc bends around. Whether the *níð* was false is a question **each route answers differently and canon never settles** (C-GRIM-04). She may appear among the straw-dead in the Benevolent God ending (`07`) — but even there she is a *seat filled*, not a mystery solved. She is the game's thesis-in-a-person: a life overwritten by carved words (C-THEME-01).

**Key relationships.** Wife/queen of **Ragnvald** · sister of **Gunnhild** · mother of **Grím** · slandered with **Loki** by **Hrafnmark's** verse — the lie that makes **Hel** a possible kinswoman.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *Was the níð true?* Never confirmed (C-GRIM-04). The surviving stave (C-NID-EVIDENCE-01) proves the *commission* — that Ragnvald paid — which proves the verse was **bought**, not that its content was **false**. The player can prove she was *framed* without ever proving she was *innocent*. This distinction is deliberate and load-bearing.
- *Ulfheim's Aldís may be as invented as the níð's.* Gunnhild's banner-myth is a carving too (see Gunnhild's secrets). No source about Aldís is clean.

## Yrsa [P]
*Rename-Table label: Yrsa — Dag's sister, hanged at the last great blót.*

- **Role:** Blót-victim; Dag's engine of grief; the old faith's blood (C-DAG-01, C-LIE-05).
- **Age:** A young woman; **hanged Y−6** (Timeline §4).
- **Faction / Allegiance:** Thrall-born — present at the old faith's altar only as its victim.

**Appearance.** Remembered, not shown. The world carries the **fact of her rope** and little else — a young thrall-woman led uphill in a column, ravens on the hanging-tree. Dag carries a fuller image no one else can verify. Any depiction is secondhand: blót-witness testimony, or a brother's memory polished by grief.

**Personality & Voice.** Quoted, never living. Dag carries her words; the world carries only the fact of her death. When her "voice" appears it is Dag's memory of it — and in committed canon (C-SAINT-02) even her death was re-storied: the church sings her as the fire-martyr "Ingunn," burying the rope.

**Backstory.** Hanged at the **Blót of Yrsa** (Y−6, Timeline §4) — the last royally-sanctioned great blót, sanctioned by King **Ragnvald** and presided by **Goði Þorgeir**. She was chosen because she was **thrall-born and disposable**: a jarl's daughter would not do (C-DAG-01). The old faith did not hang her in savage frenzy; it hanged her in orderly, lawful piety, which is worse.

**Arc / narrative function.** The wound that made Dag. A **posthumous stake** whose meaning both faiths fight to own: the old faith calls her a gift to the gods, the new faith an argument for its own righteousness. Recontextualized in Ch3 when the new altar proves just as hungry (C-LIE-03) — her death was not a bug in the old order the new order fixes; it was the *engine* both orders run on.

**Key relationships.** Sister of **Dag** · killed by **Þorgeir's** rite, sanctioned by **Ragnvald** · chosen *by name* (someone picked her) · unknowing twin-in-fate of **Ingunn**.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *She was owed her freedom.* Records may show her name was chosen the very season she was due manumission — a debt the rope erased. A quiet, brutal reveal available in Ch2–3 archive/dialogue.
- *She is Ingunn.* **Committed canon** (C-SAINT-01/02, OQ-BIBLE-01 resolved): the new church's founding saint "Ingunn the Unburnt" is Yrsa — her body, spirited from the Y−6 grove, renamed, and re-storied as an ancient martyr. The old faith hanged her; the new faith sainted her corpse. The **player knows from early on**; the **characters** (Dag first) do not, until the Ch3–4 reveal (C-SAINT-03) — the game's biggest shock.

## Ingunn "the Unburnt" [P]
*Rename-Table label: Ingunn ("the Unburnt") — martyred saint, relics cocoon the re-homed hunger.*

- **Role:** The new church's founding saint — **a cult-name for Yrsa's re-storied corpse** (C-SAINT-01/02); the hunger's most beautiful door (C-LIE-03).
- **Age:** The cult *claims* an ancient martyr (~three generations gone, ~Y−100); the truth is Yrsa, **hanged Y−6** (C-SAINT-01). "Ingunn" has no separate life — she is a story laid over a dead girl.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** New church (its founding vessel). A murdered thrall-girl of the *old* faith, consumed and then re-consecrated by the new.

**Appearance.** Liturgical legend only — a hagiographer's carving, not a person. The church shows her as an incorrupt relic: a girl the grave will not spoil, laid in reliquary at the shrine Bótólfr tends, a "radiance" reported at her tomb. That radiance is the **feeding mistaken for grace** (C-LIE-03). Like Aldís and Yrsa, her real face is overwritten — here, overwritten *and then gilded.*

**Personality & Voice.** Spoken about, never speaking. Her "life" is a hagiography other men wrote and the false comfort of relic-visions. **Word-fame made flesh, then made relic.** If she ever "speaks," it is scripture quoting her — a mouth moved by the institution that consumed her.

**Backstory.** The church tells of a **freed thrall-girl** martyred at the high grove three generations gone, the blót-fire "would not take her" — the miracle. **It is a fabrication.** The incorrupt body enshrined as Ingunn is **Yrsa** (C-SAINT-01/02): hanged at the Blót of Yrsa (Y−6), her corpse spirited from the grove, renamed, relocated, and dated ancient to found the relic-cult that crystallized Y−6→Y−2 and **enabled Ragnvald's conversion**. "Unburnt" is triply true — miracle-lie, un-consumed cocoon, and *she was hanged, not burned.* By Ch3 the re-homed hunger is **cocooned in her body** (C-LIE-03): a faith that venerates dying-for-god is a cleaner harvest engine than a grove — and it was founded on a girl the grove already killed.

**Arc / narrative function.** The "saint" whose glorified death is the perfect harvest vessel — and who is secretly **Yrsa** (C-SAINT-02). Two reveals land on this body: that the relic feeds (C-LIE-03), and that the relic is Dag's murdered sister (C-SAINT-03). The player carries the second from early on; the characters get it in Ch3–4. She is not a mirror of Yrsa — she **is** Yrsa: one thrall-girl both altars killed, "both altars have blood" (C-LIE-05) collapsed into one body.

**Key relationships.** Founding saint of the church **Dag** perfects and **Bótólfr** serves · **Dag's own hanged sister**, unrecognized under the cult-name (C-SAINT-03) · object of **Bótólfr's** sincere veneration · unwitting host of the hunger **Salgerð** wants dislodged.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *"Unburnt" reads three ways.* Holy miracle (the lie), un-consumed cocoon (C-LIE-03) — and the buried truth, **she was hanged, not burned** (C-SAINT-01). The fire-miracle exists to explain away a body with no burns.
- *The relic is Yrsa.* **Committed** (C-SAINT-02): the founding saint is Dag's murdered sister, renamed and dated ancient. The player is shown it early; the reveal to the characters (Ch3–4, the opening of the body) is the game's biggest shock (C-SAINT-03).
- *Holy or merely useful-dead?* Whether the girl was ever touched by anything is unrecoverable; the *radiance* at her tomb is the feeding mistaken for grace — its origin ambiguous, its function certain: it feeds.

# III. The Five Clan Leaders

> Leaders match the institutions in `01-story-bible.md §3` and the faith/fire forks there; this file holds the *person*, that file holds the *clan*. All are placeholders (C-CLAN-LEAD-01).

## Gunnhild "the Wolf-Widow" [P]
*Rename-Table label: Gunnhild ("the Wolf-Widow") — Ulfheim leader; Aldís's sister, Grím's aunt.*

- **Role:** Ulfheim (Wolf) clan leader; wants a banner, not a kinsman (C-CLAN-LEAD-01).
- **Age:** ~44.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** Ulfheim — the outlawed remnant of Aldís's unmade kin.

**Appearance.** Weather-cured and wolf-lean, grey coming into dark hair she does not bother to hide. Skirmisher's kit: layered wool and hide, a seax and a light axe, nothing that jingles. A widow's iron ring. She has her sister's bones — which is a knife she uses on Grím the first time he sees her, because he is looking at a face half his mother's and she knows it.

**Personality & Voice.** Blunt, testing, cold-warm. She speaks of Aldís as a *cause*, not a sister, and measures loyalty in **use**, not blood — and says so to Grím's face. Every kindness she offers is also recruitment; she is not lying about the warmth, but the warmth has a purpose and she will not pretend otherwise. The most honest recruiter in the game, which is its own kind of trap.

**Backstory.** Aldís's younger sister and Grím's maternal aunt (C-CLAN-LEAD-01). When the *níð* unmade her sister (Y−20), her kin were outlawed and scattered; she gathered the remnant into the Ulfheim night-fighters and has spent twenty years turning a murdered queen into a banner. Widowed (the by-name ties her loss to the Wolf totem).

**Arc / narrative function.** **Ally or atrocity.** Courting her exposes Grím's blood early and hard (the faith fork, `01 §3.1`); the ch1-08 split (C-ROUTE-02) is the king ordering Grím to *burn her clan*. Route-dependent, she either **sees the person** or is **proved right that he was only ever a banner**. She dramatizes "a banner, not a kinsman": to court the clan is to put the cause before the boy.

**Key relationships.** Sister of **Aldís** · aunt of **Grím** · rival-ally of **Brandr's** landless host · bitter enemy of **Hrafnmark**, who carved the *níð* · unmade alongside Aldís by **Ragnvald's** verse.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *Her Aldís is a carving too.* Her account of her sister is a curated banner-myth, no truer than the *níð* — and **she privately doubts her sister was innocent** (see Aldís's secrets). Revealed at high Ulfheim trust or in the Ch1-08 aftermath: the wronged clan's holy version is *also* propaganda.
- *She has already chosen the battle where she would let Grím die.* To make him a martyr-banner, worth more dead and pure than alive and compromising. A late-Ch1 / Ch2 betrayal-tier reveal that turns her honesty into a colder thing.

## Rúnolfr "the Rune-Heir" [P]
*Rename-Table label: Rúnolfr ("the Rune-Heir") — Hrafnmark leader; níð-heir; male seiðmaðr.*

- **Role:** Hrafnmark (Raven) clan leader — master seiðmaðr and rune-lawyer; heir to the *níð* that unmade Grím's house (C-CLAN-LEAD-01, C-NID-EVIDENCE-01).
- **Age:** ~50.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** Hrafnmark — seiðr-keepers and rune-lawyers.

**Appearance.** Spare, precise, ink-stained fingers and a rune-carver's small scars on the thumb. He keeps himself immaculate in a way that reads as *argument* — a man who bears the *ergi* stigma (glossary) and refuses to shrink under it, dressing and standing as if daring the room to say it. A distaff or staff of office he handles like a scholar, not a warrior. Cold, amused eyes.

**Personality & Voice.** Precise, lawyerly, faintly amused. He treats a killing-poem as a **craft well executed** — he never apologizes, sometimes explains, and openly enjoys that you cannot answer him in his own medium. He is ruthless with the very stigma he carries: a male seiðr-worker who has made himself untouchable by being the best, and who despises weakness in others who wield the art. The most intellectually dangerous person Grím meets.

**Backstory.** Heir to the Hrafnmark rune-lawyers who carved Aldís's *níð* on the throne's commission (Y−20, Timeline §4). His house's whole prestige is that its poems *work* — that a carved verse can unmake a queen by law (C-GRIM-03). He inherited the craft, the reputation, and the **evidence** (C-NID-EVIDENCE-01).

**Arc / narrative function.** The **ugliest alliance in the game**: court your family's assassins for their arts (`01 §3.2`). Trust unlocks Seiðmaðr, Norn-touched, and Rune-reckoner; at high trust it yields the **original *níð*-stave and the commission-tally naming Ragnvald as payer** (C-NID-EVIDENCE-01) — the single greatest lever toward the King of Men road, at the cost of Grím's honor (allying with the carvers is itself *níð*-worthy). The **fire fork destroys the stave forever**: you loot ash-tongued diminished arts but lose the only proof your mother was framed.

**Key relationships.** Heir to the forebears who carved **Aldís's** *níð* on **Ragnvald's** commission · loathed by **Gunnhild** and Ulfheim · professional verse-rival of **Skapti** and the Hjortdal Skalds (law vs. *níð*, the two word-powers).

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *He keeps the receipt.* The original *níð*-stave and the commission-tally — proof Aldís was framed and that the **throne paid** (C-NID-EVIDENCE-01). Yielded only at high Hrafnmark trust; the crux of the King of Men lever.
- *He covets Grím.* He suspects Grím is the Wyrdless and wants him as the **ultimate rune-subject** — the one fate a poem might not be able to fix, and therefore the one worth carving most. His interest in Grím is never wholly an alliance; it is also appetite.

## Bǫðvarr "Bear-Sark" [P]
*Rename-Table label: Bǫðvarr ("Bear-Sark") — Bjarnholt leader; oath-bound lodge-master.*

- **Role:** Bjarnholt (Bear) clan leader — berserker lodge-master; easiest clan to win, easiest to lose (C-CLAN-LEAD-01, C-CLAN-02).
- **Age:** ~38.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** Bjarnholt — the berserker lodge.

**Appearance.** Huge, scarred, loud in his body before he says a word — a bear-champion built like the legend the by-name invokes. Bear-sark pelt across the shoulders, the lodge's rite worn as a garment. Hands that have broken more cups in oath-swearing than in anger. When the berserkergang rises, his eyes change in a way the other characters have started, quietly, to find frightening.

**Personality & Voice.** Loud, plain, generous, absolute. Oath-heavy, no irony. Open contempt for lawyers, merchants, and priests of *either* god. A broken word is a **personal wound** he answers with axes. He is the least deceptive person in the game — which is exactly why he is the easiest to lose: he cannot hedge, cannot forgive a slight, cannot pretend an oath is anything but total.

**Backstory.** Lodge-master of a berserker hall cornered and enraged by Ragnvald's raiding ban (Y−2, Timeline §4), which outlawed his young men overnight and left their fury nowhere lawful to go. His whole institution runs on hospitality-oath and shared fury (`01 §3.3`).

**Arc / narrative function.** **Fast friend, fast enemy.** A shared oath and a raid-target win him fast and grant Berserker and Wyrmslayer in full — but trust is brittle (betrayal fires below −25 and flips the whole lodge instantly, C-CLAN-02), and his oaths bind *Grím* to *his* feuds. His rage is both weapon and liability — a resource the ban created and the new order will spend. The **fire fork** stamps **OATHBREAKER** ("he broke the guest-cup"), the deepest Norse stigma, tanking starting trust with every other clan.

**Key relationships.** Lodge-brother-in-arms, sometimes allied to **Brandr's** landless host · despises **Rúnolfr's** word-craft and **Auðr's** ledger alike · potential shield or first casualty of Grím's choices.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *His lodge already raids illegally.* Despite the ban, they've taken outlaw raiding work — a knife the crown can hold to his throat.
- *He knows the ban is right — and cannot say so.* He privately grants that the raiding *rot* was real (C-LIE-04's logic applied to a warrior) but cannot admit it without unmaking himself and his lodge. And **his berserkergang is starting to feel like something that will not switch off** — an early, deniable touch of the hunger (foreshadowing Brandr, the einherjar, and the Successor).

## Auðr "the Deep-Counseled" [P]
*Rename-Table label: Auðr ("the Deep-Counseled") — Hvalfjorð leader; prime-signed merchant.*

- **Role:** Hvalfjorð (Whale) clan leader — prime-signed merchant; sells to both altars, including Grím's location (C-CLAN-LEAD-01).
- **Age:** ~46.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** Hvalfjorð — prime-signed merchant converts. Her true allegiance is the ledger.

**Appearance.** Prosperous and unshowy in the way of real wealth — good wool, a single excellent ring, a purse she never seems to touch because she never needs to. A merchant's steady, appraising look that prices everything, grief and treason included. She wears the prime-signing cross when a Christian port is watching and does not when it isn't; the cross is inventory.

**Personality & Voice.** Courteous, ledgered, never raised. She frames even betrayal as **"clearing an account."** Everything — loyalty, grief, treason — arrives itemized and priced. She is not cruel; she is *exact*. The most dangerous thing about her is that she will tell you the truth about what your life is worth on the open market, to your face, kindly.

**Backstory.** A prime-signed fleet-owner (baptism-lite for market access, historically real *primsigning*; after the real Auðr djúpúðga, a wealthy Christian-leaning ship-owner). She built a network that sells ships, grain, intelligence, and exotic gear to **both altars** (`01 §3.4`) — and holds a **standing Crown offer for Grím's location** as an open line on her books.

**Arc / narrative function.** **Loyalty is a running account.** Kept loyal, she is the game's **logistics spine** (map/market access, the Varangian/Læknir brokerage); neglected, she is the leak that ends Grím — she auctions his location to the Crown. High trust buys her **silence** (closes the Crown offer). The **fire fork** loots diminished arts plus one-time plunder but severs the intelligence/shop network and teaches every clan that Grím robs his own creditors (**MARKET-BURNER**).

**Key relationships.** Buys and sells to every faction — **Dag's** church, old-faith holds, White-God bishops — with equal courtesy · despised by **Bǫðvarr** as a coin-priest · broker between **Grím** and the wider world's goods and rumors.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *She already knows more of the Harvest Lie than she admits.* She traded for it and priced it too high to say aloud — and privately thinks the relic-cult is a **bad harvest bet**, without knowing how literally true that is (C-LIE-03). A Ch2–3 reveal at high trust.
- *She believes in no god, only the ledger.* Her prime-signing is pure commerce. This is not cynicism the game punishes — she is honest, useful, and correct about a great deal; she simply reads reality as a balance sheet, and is unnervingly good at it.

## Skapti "the Lawspeaker" [P]
*Rename-Table label: Skapti ("the Lawspeaker") — Hjortdal leader; Lawspeaker of the Thing.*

- **Role:** Hjortdal (Stag) clan leader — Lawspeaker of the Thing; gatekeeper of the King of Men trial arc (C-CLAN-LEAD-01, C-THING-01, C-CLAN-03).
- **Age:** ~58.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** Hjortdal — horse-lords who keep the Thing and the law. His allegiance is to precedent, which is both his virtue and his indictment.

**Appearance.** Old, upright, unhurried — a horse-lord who still rides well and a lawspeaker who has memorized more law than any three men. Plain, fine clothing; a staff of office he leans on more from ceremony than need. A face that has spent decades composing itself into the neutrality of judgment. He looks at Grím the way a man looks at a debt he ratified long ago.

**Personality & Voice.** Measured, formal, precedent-citing. Slow to move and immovable once moved. He answers passion with procedure and believes procedure is the **only wall between men and the wolves**. His slowness is not cowardice; it is knowledge — he knows exactly how easily law becomes the winner's poem carved deeper, because he once let it be.

**Backstory.** Lawspeaker of the Thing and horse-lord (C-CLAN-LEAD-01). **He spoke the law that ratified Aldís's unmaking** (Y−20, C-THING-01) — the *níð* was procedurally lawful, and he said so. He has kept the law and the Thing-plain for the decades since, and has never stopped knowing what that one ruling cost.

**Arc / narrative function.** **The King of Men road.** Only law can lift Grím's *vargdropi* status, so the trial arc runs through him (C-THING-01, C-END-01); taking Hjortdal by fire **forecloses that ending forever** (C-CLAN-03, FIXED). His arc's question: *will he let law correct a poem, or is law only the winner's poem carved deeper?* Law that freed no one once is now asked to free Grím — and to do it, Skapti must **indict his own past ruling**. He is custodian of the Riddari line and the Skald line, whose trust-75 **Lawspeaker** tier resolves OQ-2 and is embodied in his person.

**Key relationships.** Keeper of the Thing where Grím's fate can be legally rewritten · institutional counterweight to King **Ragnvald** · verse-rival of **Rúnolfr** (law vs. *níð*, the two word-powers) · bound by precedent to the very ruling that erased **Aldís**.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *He ratified the Unmaking* (C-THING-01). Lifting Grím's status means publicly correcting his own decades-old ruling — law used once as a murder weapon, asked now to be a remedy. The player learns his complicity as the trial arc opens (Ch3–4).
- *He knows it was lawful and rotten.* He understands the *níð* against Aldís was **procedurally clean and morally monstrous**, and that this is the law's deepest shame. His slowness *is* that knowledge. The reveal reframes his caution from weakness into a kind of penance.

# IV. The Crown & the Two Altars

## Ragnvald [P]
*Rename-Table label: Ragnvald — converted High King; Grím's tester and legal father. Opt. by-name "the White-Signed."*

- **Role:** High King — the convert ban-king; Grím's tester at ch1-08 and legal father; commissioner of Aldís's *níð* (C-KING-01/02, C-WORLD-03, C-ROUTE-02).
- **Age:** ~55.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** The Crown / new-faith convert. Torn, sincerely, between two altars.

**Appearance.** A king aging into weariness rather than frailty — still commanding, but a man who no longer expects to sleep well. The White-God cross worn openly over older, half-abandoned finery; a court caught between two liturgies, mirrored on the king's own body. Eyes that hold every erasure he has ordered and have found a reason for each. He does not look like a monster. That is the point.

**Personality & Voice.** Weary, statesmanlike, courteous, **self-forgiving** — a convert torn between two altars (shadowing Hákon the Good). He speaks of *necessity* and *the realm*, has a reason for every erasure, **believes them**, and **never gloats**. He is the sincerest voice for the argument the game refuses to let be cartoonish: that a monstrous act can buy a real peace, and that the man who orders it can be neither villain nor fool.

**Backstory.** A convert-king whose reign is **built of erasures** (C-KING-01): he commissioned the *níð* that unmade his own queen **Aldís** (Y−20), sanctioned the last great blót at which **Yrsa** was hanged (Y−6), and banned raiding on his conversion (Y−2), outlawing a generation — the Landless **Brandr** will lead. Each act, to him, bought peace, order, or a future.

**Arc / narrative function.** A reign built of erasures whose one loose thread is **Grím** — whose living presence reopens the single question Ragnvald paid twenty years to close forever (C-KING-02). At **ch1-08** he sets the test: prove the rumor means nothing by razing your mother's kin yourself (C-ROUTE-02) — the father unknowingly ordering the son to burn the mother's kin (C-GRIM-04). He is the **human-scale mirror of the new faith**: right about the wound, monstrous in the cure (C-KING-01, C-LIE-04).

**Key relationships.** Husband-king of **Aldís** · legal father who disowned **Grím** (biological paternity unconfirmed) · master, then predecessor, of **Dag** — who learns statecraft from him and surpasses him at it.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *Grím may be his own disowned son* (C-GRIM-04, C-KING-02) — canon never confirms it. Razing Ulfheim also conveniently removes a **rival claim to his throne**: the test is mercy-play, self-interest, and paternal dread at once.
- *He is not sure the new faith is true.* He knows only that the **old one is finished**. His conversion is statecraft that may also be genuine — even he cannot fully separate the two, which is precisely why he is not cartoon-evil (C-KING-01).

## Goði Þorgeir [P]
*Rename-Table label: Þorgeir — goði at the grove (Ch1); the old priesthood's face.*

- **Role:** Goði at the grove — the old faith's sincere human face; hanged Yrsa; presides the escalating illegal blóts (C-DAG-01, C-WORLD-05, OQ-5, C-LIE-01/02).
- **Age:** ~60.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** Old priesthood.

**Appearance.** A grave, dignified old priest in the vestments of a dying order — grove-stained wool, iron and amber, the tools of the blót carried with liturgical care. His hands are steady at the rope in a way that is meant to read as reverence, not cruelty, because to him it *is* reverence. He looks like exactly what he believes he is: a shepherd of souls, not a butcher.

**Personality & Voice.** Grave, liturgical, self-certain — the certainty of a man **never once shown the bottom of his own rite**. He frames each escalation as necessity and each offering as a gift, wounded rather than wicked, defending a dying order against apostasy. And the order he defends is **not only a rope and a lie**: he can plead — truly, and be right — that the old faith bound free kindreds into kin, ancestor-honor, and the law of the Thing itself, a real belonging the White God's market-faith dissolves (C-LIE-05a). He wins that half of the argument and loses the altar, and both are true at once. He is the old faith's sincerity made flesh: he means every word, which is exactly what makes him **useful to the hunger** (C-LIE-02).

**Backstory.** Presided over the **Blót of Yrsa** (Y−6, Timeline §4) — sanctioned then by Ragnvald — and now over the **illegal, escalating post-ban blóts** (C-WORLD-05, OQ-5), a desperate priesthood answering the king's conversion with unsanctioned human offerings. He has served the valkyrie doctrine (C-LIE-01) his whole life, believing the harvest rises to Valhalla.

**Arc / narrative function.** The old faith's **sincere face** and its clearest indictment: he serves the Corpse-Eater unknowing (C-LIE-02), while the priesthood's inner rite half-knows the descent and calls it "seeding the roots" (C-LIE-02a, glossary). By Ch3 he may glimpse that **the harvest goes down**, and either break or dig in — a possible late witness to the Lie who cannot bear it.

**Key relationships.** Killed **Yrsa** (Dag's undying enemy) · sanctioned in his last great rite by **Ragnvald** · doctrinal enemy of **Bótólfr** and the new church · unwitting servant of the **Corpse-Eater** and, through it, of **Hel's** grievance.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *He knows the sacrifice was class, not piety.* He chose a thrall-born girl because a jarl's daughter could not be offered (C-DAG-01) — and buries it. Available as a Ch1–3 reveal that denies the old faith any noble-savage defense (C-LIE-05).
- *He suspects the blót never fed Valhalla* and buries that too. **His faith is real, which is exactly what makes him useful to the hunger.** If he is ever forced to see the bottom of his rite (Ch3), he is a man who may shatter.

## Bótólfr [P]
*Rename-Table label: Bótólfr — representative new-faith cleric.*

- **Role:** Representative sincere White-God priest — genuinely frees thralls; tends Ingunn's shrine unaware of what nests in it (C-CLERIC-01, C-LIE-03/04, C-SAINT-01).
- **Age:** ~35.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** New church (White God).

**Appearance.** Plain, kind-faced, deliberately poor in dress — a working priest, not a prelate. Manumission-cords and a simple White-God cross; hands that have done real labor beside the thralls he frees. A warmth in the face that is completely genuine and completely disarming. He looks trustworthy because he *is* — and that trust is the cocoon's camouflage (C-CLERIC-01).

**Personality & Voice.** Warm, plain, pastoral, scriptural. He quotes the martyr and argues the new faith **honestly and well — and wins the argument**, which is the point (C-LIE-04). He is not naive about cruelty; he has seen the old faith's altar and rightly condemns it. His **sincerity is the trap**: the new faith deserves loyalty on his account, and that earned loyalty is what the hunger hides behind.

**Backstory.** A convert-priest who **genuinely manumits thralls** (C-LIE-04) and tends the **shrine of Ingunn the Unburnt** (C-SAINT-01), unaware the re-homed hunger has cocooned in her relics (C-LIE-03). He is the new faith's honest human face — the living proof that its virtue is real.

**Arc / narrative function.** The **human proof the new faith deserves loyalty** — his goodness is the perfect camouflage for what he unknowingly tends. A possible **Ch3–4 horror** when he glimpses what feeds at Ingunn's tomb: the "radiance" is the harvest mistaken for grace, and he is, in effect, an **unwitting slaughterhouse chaplain**. He may be the one who, late and horrified, **helps prove the Lie**.

**Key relationships.** Serves the church **Dag** climbs — instrument, then discard, of Dag's cold statecraft · keeper of **Ingunn's** relic-cult · doctrinal enemy of **Þorgeir** · beloved by the thralls he frees.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *His shrine harvests better than any blót.* Freed thralls who "die in the White God's grace" at Ingunn's tomb are harvested cleaner than the grove ever managed (C-LIE-03); the radiance is the feeding. Revealed to the player Ch3→4; whether Bótólfr sees it, and when, is a scene-level lever.
- *His sincerity cuts both ways.* If he ever fully believes what he preaches about dying-for-god, he becomes the hunger's **best priest**. His goodness is not a shield against the horror; it is the door.

# V. The Dead's Petition

## Salgerð "the Grey-Guest" [P]
*Rename-Table label: Salgerð ("the Grey-Guest") — Hel's recurring emissary; a dead völva.*

- **Role:** Hel's one recurring emissary — dreams and corpse-roads; gates the True ledger and the Benevolent God / Successor content (C-EMISSARY-01, C-LIE-06, C-ALIGN-02).
- **Age:** Dead; appears ageless.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** Hel / the un-glorious, straw-dead.

**Appearance.** A dead völva walking the corpse-roads in a dream: grey — the by-name's colour — road-dust and grave-cold, a seeress's staff and beads worn thin. She has the stillness of someone with nowhere left to hurry to. She arrives the way an uninvited guest arrives at a threshold: courteously, unavoidably, and never quite where the door was a moment ago.

**Personality & Voice.** Soft, patient, grief-worn, formal — a **courtroom-of-the-dead cadence**. She speaks in the grammar of **roads, thresholds, owed-debts and lawful shares, and doors that do not open twice**; never in threats. Her grief is a **bureaucrat's grief for a broken order** — which is exactly what keeps her from ever being a simple tempter. She **cannot lie, but she can withhold** (C-EMISSARY-01), and the shape of what she leaves unsaid is her most dangerous instrument.

**Backstory.** A dead völva bound to Hel's service (C-EMISSARY-01). She walks Grím's dreams and the corpse-roads pleading that the harvest be returned to Hel's **lawful course** — not out of malice, but because **Hel's halls are starving** while the Corpse-Eater and now the new church divert the dead downward and inward (C-LIE-06). Hers is a lawsuit over who eats the dead, not a temptation to evil.

**Arc / narrative function.** She **escalates from dream-omen to guide** of the Benevolent God / Successor routes. Her lawful plea and Grím's hunger can converge: the **Benevolent God** ending is her petition *granted* (Grím takes the empty seat as god of the straw-dead, Hel's halls fed, Salgerð at rest); the **Successor** ending is her petition **perverted into its opposite** (Grím feeds the hunger instead, and even Hel is left to starve). She gates the True ledger (C-ALIGN-02, OQ-3) — she judges what *happened*, not what was *sung*.

**Key relationships.** Emissary of **Hel** · recurring presence in **Grím's** dreams · if the *níð* was true, a servant of Grím's own kinswoman (C-GRIM-04).

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *She may be someone Grím lost.* She could be one of Aldís's dead, or someone else Grím has grieved — a withholding, not a lie. A slow Ch2–3 tease that the player may never fully resolve.
- *Her "lawful harvest" is self-interested.* Restoring the balance feeds **Hel**, not the world; she is a petitioner for one broken institution against another, not a savior. Honest about the debt, quiet about the appetite behind it.
- *In the Successor ending she is the first hope Grím betrays.* Hel, too, goes hungry — the grey guest turned away at the last door.

# VI. The Landless

## Brandr [P]
*Rename-Table label: Brandr — leader of the outlawed-raider host (Wiegraf-analog).*

- **Role:** Captain of the outlawed-raider host — the Landless; introduced ch1-04 "The Landless" (C-LANDLESS-01).
- **Age:** ~28.
- **Faction / Allegiance:** The Landless — the outlawed young warriors the raiding ban discarded. A movement, not a clan (`01 §3` cross-cut).

**Appearance.** Young for his weight of grievance, bright-eyed and battle-worn, a captain who dresses no better than the poorest man in his host on purpose — the firebrand of the by-name made flesh. Mismatched war-gear taken where it could be taken; a good sword he did not inherit. He has the physical magnetism of a man people follow toward death, and some part of him knows it and uses it.

**Personality & Voice.** Rousing, wounded, magnetic, absolute. He speaks for the erased in **fornyrðislag cadences** (C-TONE-01) and **means every word** — the most sympathetic sword ever pointed at the player, which is exactly what makes him dangerous. He is right, and his rightness is curdling; the game must never let the player dismiss him as a fanatic, only mourn what he becomes.

**Backstory.** An idealist the raiding ban (Y−2, Timeline §4) turned into a criminal overnight, along with a whole generation of landless young men (C-LANDLESS-01). He gathered the discarded into a host and fights for them — genuinely right that the ban outlawed the poor and young for being poor and young. Distinct from but allied to Clan Ulfheim, sharing Bjarnholt's fury from the outlaw side.

**Arc / narrative function.** The **Wiegraf arc**: idealist → zealot → betrayed and broken → an **early vessel the hunger works through** — a cautionary mirror of what Grím could become if grievance curdles into hunger, foreshadowing the einherjar and the Successor (C-DEATH-02). He is the foil-in-miniature to Grím's four-way choice: the righteous cause the deeper hunger exploits precisely *because* it is righteous.

**Key relationships.** Rival-ally of **Gunnhild's** Ulfheim; shares **Bǫðvarr's** fury from the outlaw side · speaks for a generation **Ragnvald's** ban outlawed · foil-in-miniature to **Grím** · possible pawn or partner of **Salgerð's** cause.

**Secrets & reveals.**
- *Some of his host are just killers.* He knows a portion of his men use the cause as cover, and he will take a **martyr's death deliberately** — not knowing, or not caring, that dying-for-a-cause **feeds the harvest** (C-LIE-02).
- *The bargain's bait is the Lie's exact bait.* What he is drawn toward promises the Landless a **hall in death that Valhalla denies them** — the precise shape of the Harvest Lie's sales pitch. He may return as an **einherji the player must unhook** (C-DEATH-02): the martyr's reward was always a mouth to be fed to.
