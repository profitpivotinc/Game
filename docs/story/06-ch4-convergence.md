## Chapter Epigraph

> Roads run together    where the reckoning waits;
> law binds or breaks    on the level plain;
> the sealed saint opens,    and the sooth stands bare —

> the maw minds well    what the mouth forgot;

## Chapter-Open — the Skald

**[STAGE: the hall, ~Y+300. The SKALD feeds the fire himself, as he has every chapter; no thrall comes, none ever comes (C-FRAME-04). By now the seams show plainer — the mead has not warmed all evening, the fire throws light and no smoke, the far benches have not thinned by a single body though the night is old, and not one of them has risen to find the door. He does not remark on it. Neither, quite yet, should you (C-WORLD-02, C-FRAME-03). Present tense, to the benches (C-TONE-03).]**

> **SKALD:** Spring, then — the last spring in the song, though he did not know it, and I will not tell you yet how I do. Two winters he had been two men on two roads, the crown's hound and the crown's quarry, and this is the spring the roads run back into one. That is what a convergence is: the saga gathering its threads to the one knot it has been tying since the first strand.
>
> Three things happen on this plain, and you will judge all three. A law is asked to unsay itself — the same law, the same old mouth, that once said a queen out of the world. *(the pause where a name should be, and does not come)* …her whom the Thing unnamed; you know I cannot give her to you. And a crown comes down on a brow that fate fixed long before the boy at the anvil was ever a rumor — Dag was always going to be king, in every telling of this I have ever sung, and I have sung it more times than you would believe of a hall this small.
>
> And a box is opened. *(quieter)* Mark that one. A holy box, sealed and gilded and carried three hundred miles from where its bones were made, kept shut so long that shutting it *was* the worship — and in this spring a blade goes into the seam and the lid comes up. I have sung the saint many nights. I have never once sung what was in the box. Tonight you will see it before the men in the story do, because you have known since the second winter and they never let themselves look.
>
> After the box, the road goes down. It always went down; you have seen that now. What he *was* when he reached the bottom — that the four of you will settle before the fire burns low, for there are four ways to end a man and only one of them is his own.
