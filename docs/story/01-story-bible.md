## 1. World & Geography

### 1.1 The Northlands at a glance

The **Northlands** (C-WORLD-01) present as grounded ~10th-century Scandinavia in the conversion era: a coast-and-fell country where every livable acre is wrung from the land between the sea-cliffs and the ice. It is not a fantasy kingdom with magic on tap. Magic is rare, ambiguous, and feared (C-WORLD-02); the mythic layer beneath — the Corpse-Eater, Hel, the Wyrd — surfaces only gradually and is fully undeniable only by Chapter 3. A player walking the world in Chapter 1 should read it as *history*, not myth.

Four terrain registers organize the map roster and the fiction (they map onto the terrain vocabulary in D-TERR-01):

**Fjord coasts.** The populated edge. Ship-nausts, longhouses, herring-racks, and steep shingle beaches under rock walls. This is where the new faith arrived — by keel, through merchants (§4.2) — and where raiding launched from before the ban. Coastal holds are wealth measured in ships and salt. The tutorial and the early Chapter 1 battles live here (ch1-01 *Strandhögg*, ch1-02 *Longhouse Night*): **cliffs teach elevation and bow-range** because the whole coast is a stack of ledges over water — and water has a downward pull the game will later make literal.

**Inland holds.** Turf-walled farmsteads and the halls of the landed up the valley floors — the seats of the clans (§3). Mixed field, dirt, road, and wood-floor interiors. This is the country the raiding ban (§1.3) strangled: inland younger sons had no land to inherit and no raids to win a name, so they became the Landless. The holds are where **hospitality-oath** and feud law govern life, and where a broken guest-cup is the deepest wound one can give.

**High fells & the grove.** Above the tree-line: rock, snow, ice, scree, and the sacred groves where the old faith keeps its blóts. Cold, vertical, witness-poor country — the perfect ground for things done that no one is meant to sing (the fell-ambush showcase, ch2-outlaw, lives here). The **high grove** is the old faith's altar: where Yrsa was hanged (§4.1) and where Ingunn the Unburnt was martyred a generation before (§4.2) — the one ground both faiths soaked in a thrall-girl's blood (C-SAINT-02). Keep it **distinct from the raven-wood**, Hrafnmark's inland rune-grove (§3.2, Rename Table); the high grove is the altar of the old faith, the raven-wood the seat of its rune-lawyers.

**The Thing-plain.** The **Thingvellir plain** (C-WORLD-01) where law is spoken: a wide, level assembly-ground with the law-circle at its heart, ringed by booth-camps for the season of the Thing. This is neutral, sacred-by-law ground — the one place a *vargdropi's* fate can be legally re-carved (C-THING-01) and therefore the hinge of the King of Men road. The story converges here in Chapter 4 (C-ROUTE-03), and the law-circle becomes a mechanical **no-kill zone** in the trial-battle showcase. Geographically flat, morally the steepest ground in the game.

*Terrain note for downstream map files:* the D-TERR-01 vocabulary already carries `root` and the D-OVER-01 vocabulary carries `corpse_road`, `hallowed`, and `law_circle`. These are the seams where the historical shell thins and the myth shows through; they should stay rare until Chapter 3 by design (C-WORLD-02).

### 1.2 The political situation

Three royal acts, in order (C-WORLD-03, Timeline §4, C-KING-01), made the powder-keg the game lights:

1. **The Unmaking (Y−20).** The reigning High King's queen, **Aldís**, was legally erased by a commissioned *níð*-verse alleging she lay with Loki (C-GRIM-03). Her kin were outlawed and scattered — the origin of the Ulfheim remnant. This is the crime the game is *named* for (*vargdropi* = the outlaw's barred child): a house destroyed by a poem.
2. **The Blót of Yrsa (Y−6).** The last *royally sanctioned* great blót at the high grove (OQ-5), at which a thrall-born girl was hanged because a jarl's daughter would not do (§4.1, C-DAG-01). The old faith at its most self-certain — and its most damning.
3. **The Conversion & the Ban (Y−2).** The High King took the White God's faith and **banned raiding** (C-WORLD-03, C-KING-01). At a stroke this outlawed a whole generation of landless young warriors whose only lawful road to a name — the raid — was closed. The old priesthood answered the king's apostasy with **illegal, escalating blóts** (C-WORLD-05, OQ-5).

At **game start (Y0)** these three wounds are all still open and now septic. A **conversion civil war is igniting** (C-WORLD-05): a convert-crown and a new church on one side; a dying, feral old priesthood on the other; five great clans (§3) hedging, arming, or already in open outlawry between them; and, cutting across all of it, the **Landless** — Brandr's outlawed-raider host (C-LANDLESS-01), young men the ban made criminals for being poor, whose righteous grievance is the tinder the deeper hunger will exploit.

Into this walks Grím: legally kinless, uncannily skilled, and the one living thread the Norns cannot fix (C-GRIM-05). His mere existence re-opens the one question the crown paid twenty years to close forever (C-KING-02). The war does not need him to start — it needs him to *choose a shape for its ending*.

> **Faith-nuance guardrail (applies throughout, per KICKOFF §2 / hard rules).** The ban was **right about a real rot**: raiding *was* a bloodletting, and it did stop real bloodshed. The ban was **monstrous in its cure**: it criminalized a generation for being born too late and too poor. Neither the old order nor the new is noble or cartoon-evil. Only people earn loyalty (C-LIE-04, C-LIE-05).

## 2. Theme — Word-Fame

### 2.1 The one idea

The game has exactly one thesis, stated in the *Hávamál* truth the frame opens on (C-TONE-02, C-THEME-01): **cattle die, kinsmen die, but the fame of a dead man's deeds never dies.** Everything — the frame, the *níð* plot device, the witness mechanic, the endings — is a facet of a single question: **who is remembered, who is erased, and who controls the carving.**

Word-fame is not a reward system bolted onto a war story. It *is* the war story. The civil war is, underneath the theology, a fight over **who gets to carve the record** of what the Northlands were and who was right. A poem unmade a queen (§4.1). A hagiography may be about to swallow a murdered girl (§4.2, C-SAINT-02). A saga three centuries on still cannot say Aldís's name because the law struck it out (the frame demonstrates this in its own voice — see `00-frame.md`). To the people of this world, to be *unsung* is a second, realer death; to control the carving is the only immortality on offer — and it is a weapon.

### 2.2 The two ledgers, narratively

The mechanics of the dual ledgers live in `spec-alignment.md` (C-ALIGN-01…05). Here is what they *mean* in the fiction:

- **The Sung Ledger** is the world's memory — what was witnessed and therefore can enter the saga. It is the **carved record**: partial, editable, and only ever as true as its witnesses are alive. It gates clan trust and public standing (C-ALIGN-02), because reputation is built of what people *saw*.
- **The True Ledger** is what actually happened — everything, witnessed or not. Only the dead and the powers below (Hel, the straw-dead) read it whole (C-ALIGN-02, OQ-3). It gates Hel's content and the hidden ending.

The gap between them *is* the theme, playable. A player can **massacre a clan and keep a golden name by leaving no survivors** (C-ALIGN-03) — the game never stops them; it just remembers on the ledger no living skald can read. Grið (the yield/mercy mechanic, C-ALIGN-05) and the witness rule (C-ALIGN-04) are the daily texture of this: every act of mercy or slaughter is also a decision about *who lives to sing it*. The endings then pay the theme off at full volume (§6, C-END-01): the Merciless Ruler's "golden age" is a Sung-Ledger lie built on murdered witnesses; the King of Men chooses to be remembered *smaller and truer* (True > Sung); the Successor is the record itself weaponized into the last lie ever told.

The design rule that keeps this honest: **word-fame is shown as power, never as morality.** A clean Sung Ledger is not virtue — it can be the receipt for a perfect crime. The game refuses to conflate "well-remembered" with "good." That refusal is the whole point.

## 3. The Five Clans (Institutionally)

Each clan is a **standing institution** — an identity, a totem, a region, a job-line it is custodian of, and a **faith-vs-fire fork** that decides whether Grím wins its arts by trust or loots them diminished by conquest (C-CLAN-01, C-CLAN-02). Leaders are dossiers in `02-characters.md`; this section holds the institution, not the person. The mechanics of trust scalars, exercises, and betrayal thresholds live in `spec-clans-trust.md` (C-CLAN-02).

**The fork, in general (C-CLAN-01):** the **faith path** (earning trust) grants a clan's *full* job line and its capstone; the **fire path** (taking the clan by conquest) loots a **diminished** variant — fewer abilities, no capstone, and permanent **stigma tags** in dialogue (the tag set is registered in C-CLAN-04) that follow Grím and poison starting trust elsewhere. Fire is always available, always cheaper up front, and always costs something the Sung Ledger will not forget. This is word-fame rendered as a strategy layer: every clan you burn is a verse someone else gets to carve about you.

### 3.1 Ulfheim — the Wolf

- **Totem / region:** Wolf; outlawed, no fixed hold — skirmisher camps in the inland margins and fells, the scattered remnant of Aldís's unmade kin (C-GRIM-03).
- **Identity:** Night-fighters and skirmishers who nurse a **banner-myth of Aldís** and want vengeance dressed as restoration. They measure a man by his *use to the cause*, not by blood, and say so to his face. They want **a banner, not a kinsman** (C-CLAN-LEAD-01).
- **Custodian of:** Skógarmaðr (outlaw/ninja-analog), Raider (thief-analog) (C-CLAN-01).
- **Faith fork:** Courting Ulfheim means **owning Grím's blood publicly** — they follow the royal claim, not the boy — which exposes his *vargdropi* status early and hard and endangers every other path.
- **Fire fork:** Burning Ulfheim is the Chapter 1 split itself (ch1-08, the Balmamusa pattern, C-ROUTE-02). It loots the night-arts diminished, tags Grím **KINSLAYER** ("he burned his own blood"), hardens the True Ledger toward Blood, and forecloses every Ulfheim-restoration path. The grave-goods include Aldís-linked relics that *indict* him.

### 3.2 Hrafnmark — the Raven

- **Totem / region:** Raven; **the raven-wood**, a wooded inland march of seiðr-halls and rune-workshops (distinct from the old faith's *high grove*, §1.1; Rename Table).
- **Identity:** **Seiðr-keepers and rune-lawyers** — words are their weapons. They **carved the *níð* that unmade Grím's house** (§4.1) and feel no guilt, only craft. Their male seiðr-workers bear the *ergi* stigma (glossary) and wield it anyway. This is the ugliest alliance in the game: courting your own family's assassins for their arts.
- **Custodian of:** Seiðmaðr (black-mage-analog), Norn-touched (time-mage-analog), Rune-reckoner (calculator-analog) (C-CLAN-01).
- **Faith fork:** High trust yields the **original *níð*-stave and the commission-tally naming the throne as payer** (C-NID-EVIDENCE-01) — legal proof that she was **framed** (that the verse was *bought*), not that its content was false; you can prove she was framed without ever proving her innocent (framed ≠ innocent, see `02` Aldís) — the single greatest lever toward the King of Men road. The price is honor: allying with the carvers is itself *níð*-worthy.
- **Fire fork:** Burning the **raven-wood** loots an **ash-tongued, diminished** seiðr line *and destroys the *níð*-stave forever* — you gain crippled arts but lose the only proof your mother was framed (C-NID-EVIDENCE-01; framed, not proven innocent — see `02` Aldís). Tag: **LOREBURNER** (C-CLAN-04) — the raven-wood-burner who destroyed the proof.

### 3.3 Bjarnholt — the Bear

- **Totem / region:** Bear; a fell-edge lodge-hold.
- **Identity:** A **berserker lodge**, oath above all, cornered and enraged by the raiding ban that outlawed its young men (§1.3). Hot, generous to the sworn, murderous to the forsworn. Loyalty runs entirely on **hospitality-oath and shared fury**. Easiest clan to win, easiest to lose.
- **Custodian of:** Berserker (monk-analog), Wyrmslayer (dragoon-analog) (C-CLAN-01).
- **Faith fork:** A shared oath and a raid-target win them **fast** and grant both lines in full — but their oaths bind Grím to *their* feuds, and their trust is brittle (betrayal events fire fast and flip the whole lodge instantly, per C-CLAN-02).
- **Fire fork:** Breaking the lodge loots a diminished Berserker (no capstone rage) and Wyrmslayer (no wyrm-oath finisher), and stamps **OATHBREAKER** — "he broke the guest-cup," the deepest Norse stigma — which tanks starting trust with *every other clan*.

### 3.4 Hvalfjorð — the Whale

- **Totem / region:** Whale; a prime-signed merchant harbor on the fjord coast.
- **Identity:** **Prime-signed merchant converts** — baptism-lite for market access, not faith (historically real *primsigning*; §4.2). Faith is market access; loyalty is a contract; they serve whoever pays and survives, and **invoice honestly**. They sell ships, grain, intelligence, and exotic gear to **both altars** — and hold a standing Crown offer for **Grím's location** (C-CLAN-LEAD-01).
- **Custodian of:** Varangian (samurai-analog: east-road blade arts), Læknir (chemist-analog), plus **exotic-gear brokerage** (C-CLAN-01).
- **Faith fork:** **Bought, not won.** Trust is a running account paid in grain, ships, and intelligence — the game's **logistics spine** (map and market access). High trust buys their **silence** (closes the standing Crown offer for Grím's location). They will never love him; they will keep the ledger straight.
- **Fire fork:** Seizing the harbor loots diminished Varangian/Læknir plus a one-time plunder — but **severs the intelligence and shop network** (map/market access shrinks) and teaches every clan you rob your own creditors. Tags: **MARKET-BURNER / faithless — no port will deal.**

### 3.5 Hjortdal — the Stag

- **Totem / region:** Stag; horse-lord dales adjoining the Thing-plain.
- **Identity:** **Horse-lords who keep the Thing and the law** — slow, proud, precedent-bound. The **cleanest institution** in the game, which is not the same as clean (its precedent ratified Aldís's unmaking, C-THING-01). The only clan that can act on Grím's status **by law rather than by fire**.
- **Custodian of:** Riddari (mounted knight, Hjortdal-exclusive), Skald (orator/bard fused — the **Lawspeaker** ability tier is the Skald job's trust-75 title, not a separate job, per OQ-2) (C-CLAN-01).
- **Faith fork:** The **longest, hardest courtship** — trust earned by *upholding law in play*: accepting grið publicly, sparing witnesses, keeping a clean Sung Ledger. At trust-75 it unlocks the **Lawspeaker** tier and the **trial arc that can legally unmake *vargdropi* status** — the King of Men gate (C-END-01).
- **Fire fork:** Breaking the Thing (riding down the lawspeakers) loots a diminished Riddari **and forecloses the King of Men ending permanently** (C-CLAN-03, FIXED) — because only law can unmake *vargdropi* status. Tags: **LAW-BREAKER / Thing-slayer — outlaw forever.** It is the ultimate word-fame self-erasure: destroying the one institution that could carve your name back into the law.

> **Cross-cutting: the Landless are not a clan.** Brandr's outlawed-raider host (C-LANDLESS-01) is a *movement*, not an institution — distinct from but allied to Ulfheim, sharing Bjarnholt's fury from the outlaw side. It has no job custodianship and no clan-trust scalar; it is the human face of the ban's cost and a Chapter-1 mirror of Grím's own four-way choice. Detail lives in `02-characters.md`.

## 4. The Two Faiths & The Harvest Lie

This is the game's buried spine (KICKOFF §2 pillars 2–3). The horror is **structural, not villainous**: no one who matters is a cackling cultist. Both faiths are sincerely served; both are right about something real; both altars have blood on them (C-LIE-05). The Lie is that the machinery *underneath* both is a slaughterhouse — and neither priesthood knows it.

### 4.1 The old faith

Goðar and blóts at the high grove, under the **valkyrie doctrine** (C-LIE-01): the glorious dead are borne **up** to Valhalla. Public, law-entangled, and **sincerely believed** — its face, Goði Þorgeir (§`02`), means every word, though the priesthood's inner rite half-knows the descent and calls it "seeding the roots" (C-LIE-02a, glossary).

**Its genuine good — the thing the conversion destroys (C-LIE-05a).** The old faith is not only doctrine-that-lies and an altar with blood on it. It **binds free kindreds** into a living web of kin, ancestor-honor, and communal law — the **Thing itself is old-faith-entangled**, its assemblies opened with old-faith rite — and it locates every person, high or thrall-adjacent, inside obligations that are also belonging. That belonging is real, and the White God's individualism and Hvalfjorð's market-faith (§4.2, §3.4) **dissolve** it. The old altar is **mournable-in-loss** the way the new is celebrable-in-gift; a player should be able to grieve what conversion costs even while condemning what the grove does. *(This is the hard-rule counterweight: the old faith must have a true thing to plead, or it collapses into one-dimensional villainy.)*

Its weaponized word is **seiðr and rune-craft** (Hrafnmark, §3.2): a *níð*-verse can unmake a house *by law* (Aldís, C-GRIM-03), and a male seiðr-worker carries the *ergi* stigma (glossary) — **poems as instruments of legal death.**

It is **never noble-savage'd** (C-LIE-05, hard rule): it hanged **Yrsa** because a thrall-born girl was an acceptable offering and a jarl's daughter was not (C-DAG-01). This is a class engine with an altar. After the Y−2 ban, its sanctioned great blóts became **illegal and escalating** (C-WORLD-05, OQ-5): desperate goðar answer the king's conversion with unsanctioned human offerings — a dying order turning feral as it loses the law that housed it, **not** a cartoon of savagery.

### 4.2 The new faith

The **White God** (Hvítakristr-analog, C-WORLD-04) — never named as real-world Christianity. Incoming, backed by the convert-King, and entered by merchants through **prime-signing** for market access (Hvalfjorð / §3.4). It **genuinely frees thralls** and is **genuinely right that the old ways are rotten** — right about the rot, wrong about the cure (C-LIE-04). Its honest human face, the cleric Bótólfr (§`02`), *wins a **contested** argument* against the old faith — contested, because the old faith has a true thing to plead in return (the kinship-web and belonging it holds, C-LIE-05a), and Bótólfr wins anyway, on the strength of the thralls he frees. That the better argument still costs something real is exactly the point: the new faith's virtue is genuine and its sincerity is the trap.

It is founded on the **relic-cult of Ingunn "the Unburnt"** (C-SAINT-01) — the miracle the church tells: an ancient freed thrall-girl martyred at the high grove ~Y−25 whom the blót-fire "would not take," her incorrupt relics enabling the King's conversion. **That story is a fabrication.** The venerated body is **Yrsa** — the thrall-girl hanged at the Blót of Yrsa (Y−6), spirited from the grove, renamed, relocated, and re-storied as an ancient saint (C-SAINT-02, **committed canon**). "Unburnt" reads three ways: the holy miracle (the lie), the un-consumed cocoon (C-LIE-03), and the buried truth — **she was hanged, not burned.**

Ingunn and Yrsa are **the same erased woman** (C-SAINT-02): one murdered thrall-girl claimed in turn by both altars — the old faith hanged her, the new faith sainted her corpse to found itself. "Both altars have blood" (C-LIE-05) rendered literally, in one body. The **player is shown this early** (the hanging beside the relic-cult); the **characters** — above all Dag, her brother — do not learn it until a late Ch3–4 reveal (C-SAINT-03), the game's biggest shock.

The new faith is **already colonized from inside by the hunger it believes it defeated** (KICKOFF §3): the shepherds are not in on it; its goodness is the cocoon's camouflage. **Right about the wound, monstrous in the cure — a mirror of the convert-King himself** (C-KING-01).

### 4.3 The Harvest Lie — reveal cadence

The Lie does not drop in one scene; it **cracks on a schedule** (C-WORLD-02, C-LIE-02/03), so the world stays historical until the myth is earned:

- **Chapter 1 — believed.** "Valhalla" is doctrine on *both* the recited and played levels. Downed units "rise" in the valkyrie VFX (C-DEATH-01); magic is rare and ambiguous (C-WORLD-02), so the descent is deniable. The player and the world both believe the valkyrie story.
- **Chapter 2 — cracking.** Corpse-roads, Salgerð's dreams (§5), and einherjar rumors accumulate. Grið and witness scenes show the dead **not arriving where doctrine promised.** The downward pull becomes *suspectable* but not yet provable.
- **Chapter 3 — undeniable (C-LIE-02).** The harvest goes **down** — to the **Corpse-Eater** gnawing at the **roots of the World-Tree** (Níðhöggr- and Yggdrasill-analogs; canon uses only common nouns, Rename Table). The old priesthood served it without understanding. **"Valhalla is the sales pitch of a slaughterhouse."** The **einherjar debut in enemy formations** (C-DEATH-02) and the concealed corpse-road scene make it impossible to deny.
- **Chapter 3→4 — re-homed (C-LIE-03).** The hunger is found **cocooned in Ingunn's relics inside the *new* church** — the *better* engine, because a faith that venerates dying-for-god harvests cleaner than a grove. **Dag learns this in Chapter 4 and crowns himself anyway** (C-DAG-03). Both altars are indicted — *never* balanced into "both sides bad, so nothing matters"; only people earn or lose loyalty (C-LIE-05). **The Lie does not die at conversion. It upgrades.**

The through-line: the "radiance" witnessed at Ingunn's tomb is **the feeding, mistaken for grace.** The new church is a **better slaughterhouse than the grove it replaced**, and its chaplains are unwitting.

## 5. Hel, the Corpse-Roads & the Starving Halls

**Hel** (real-myth, Loki's daughter, C-LIE-06) keeps the **un-glorious, straw-dead** — everyone Valhalla refuses. She is **not evil.** Her grievance is **justice, not malice**: her halls are **starving** because the Corpse-Eater — and now the new church — divert the dead **downward and inward**, away from her lawful share. This is a **lawsuit over who eats the dead**, an institutional dispute about a broken order, not a temptation to wickedness. That framing is what keeps her from ever being a simple dark tempter.

She operates through **dreams, corpse-roads, and one recurring emissary**, **Salgerð "the Grey-Guest"** (C-EMISSARY-01) — a dead völva who walks Grím's dreams and the corpse-roads, stating Hel's claim in the grammar of **owed-debts and lawful shares**, never in threats. She **cannot lie but can withhold.** She **gates the True Ledger and the Benevolent God / Successor content** (C-ALIGN-02). Her full dossier is in `02-characters.md`; here she matters as the *institution* of Hel's petition.

**Why her roads open to Grím:** if the *níð* against Aldís was true (never confirmed — C-GRIM-04), **Hel is Grím's kinswoman** (Loki's line). That is why two of the four endings run through her (§6):

- **Benevolent God** seats Grím as **god of the straw-dead in Hel's now-fed halls** — her petition *granted*, the empty seat taken, Salgerð finally at rest.
- **The Successor** is her petition **perverted into its opposite** — Grím feeds the hunger instead of restoring the balance, and **even Hel is left to starve.** She is the first hope he betrays.

The **corpse-roads** themselves are a terrain/overlay feature (`corpse_road`, D-OVER-01): paths the harvested dead are dragged along, *down*. They surface as a map hazard and a lore-reveal in Chapter 3 (the barrow showcase). They are the physical proof of C-LIE-02 — the road that goes the wrong way.

## 6. The Frame's Relationship to Truth (brief)

> The frame is fully owned by `00-frame.md` (C-FRAME-01…04). This is only the worldbuilding context the bible needs.

The entire game is a **saga recited by a skald ~300 years on** (C-FRAME-01), in a hall that is not what it seems. The frame is the theme's **outermost layer**: the skald's official recitation and the played events **diverge in small, deliberate ways** (the "saga remembers it differently" device, C-FRAME-02), each divergence tracked in the Saga Discrepancies table in `00-frame.md`. The skald is **deliberately never named** (C-FRAME-04), because naming the reciter would pre-empt the Successor reveal — that the hall lies under the World-Tree's roots, that it is the last night, and that the "skald" is **the harvest given a mouth.**

For the bible's purposes, three consequences matter:

1. **The record is partial and editable.** Even three centuries on, the law that unnamed Aldís still binds the skald's tongue ("her whom the law unnamed") — erasure outliving death, demonstrated in the frame's own voice. What the player learns from the three the law's redaction cannot silence — the outlawed (Gunnhild), the níð's own heir who keeps the naming stave (Rúnolfr), and the dead (Salgerð) — is what the saga cannot say.
2. **Dead witnesses make golden lines.** The frame recites atrocities as honors and slaughters as clean victories wherever the witnesses were killed (the witness mechanic dramatized at the outermost scale). The recitation **cannot be trusted about the afterlife** — it recites the Harvest Lie itself, because the skald is *inside* the slaughterhouse's sales pitch.
3. **Each ending recontextualizes the frame** (C-FRAME-03): King of Men reveals an honest, partial, mortal record; Benevolent God, an evensong of the fed dead; Merciless Ruler, a paid-for golden lie; the Successor, the last night of the world and the last line ever carved. **Who controls the carving controls which hall you think you sat in.**

## 7. Open Questions (this file)

These are worldbuilding uncertainties surfaced or relied on by this bible. Mechanical OQs (OQ-1…5) live in `CANON.md §6`; these are narrative flags for the human lead and do not contradict CANON.

- **OQ-BIBLE-01 [RESOLVED — project-lead ruling].** *Ingunn = Yrsa?* **Yes — committed** (C-SAINT-01/02/03). They are one erased woman: the relic venerated as Ingunn is Yrsa's re-storied corpse. Reconciliation that makes it hold: the ~Y−25 "ancient martyr / unburnt-by-fire" is the church's fabrication; the real body is Yrsa (Y−6), so the timeline does not double a single death.
